Growing your bonsai

You start with a two-year-old Japanese maple seedling on January 1. Time runs fast; a year passes in about 12 seconds at the fastest speed. Shape the tree by deciding where it is allowed to grow.

The calendar follows a Japanese maple in the Pacific Northwest: dormant December to February, buds break in late March, strong growth April to June, shoots harden in July and August, leaves color in October and are down by late November.

  • ✂ Prune: click anywhere on a branch or the trunk to cut it there. Everything shown in red is removed. Cutting the trunk's top makes a bud below take over as the new leader, which builds taper and movement.
  • ✋ Pinch: click a growing tip to stop it from extending, or rub off a bud you don't want.
  • ➰ Wire: click a branch or the trunk to wire it from that point to its tip, then drag a wired section to bend it: the section's tip follows your pointer, swinging about the section's base (marked with a white dot) in the plane facing you. Rotate the view and bend again to shape it in depth. Grab different points along it to add curves. Young shoots bend a lot; thick wood only a little. The bend sets over a growing season. Take the wire off too early and the unset part springs back; leave it on too long and it bites into the thickening branch and scars it. Click a wired branch to remove the wire.
  • Buds: dormant buds sit at nodes and wake up when the tips above them are removed. back buds pop up on old wood, especially after pruning. They let you build fine branching (ramification) close to the trunk.
  • Strong, unpruned shoots grow long and coarse with big leaves. Repeated cutting back gives short internodes, small leaves and dense twigs.
  • The tree grows in 3D: branches spiral around the trunk and can grow toward you, away from you, sideways, up or down. Drag to walk around the tree and check it from every side.
  • Shoots grow toward the light: up toward the sky and out toward gaps in the canopy, so most new branches angle upward or sideways.
  • New tree shows six random seedlings to choose from. Use ☆ Save form on one you like, or Save form in the side panel to keep your current tree's shape, then plant copies of it from the Saved forms tab.
  • The top and outer tips grow strongest. Crowded, shaded interior shoots weaken and die, so thin the canopy.
  • Heavy pruning in spring and summer costs health. Do big cuts in late autumn and winter, while the tree is dormant.
  • Roots and nebari: each main root thickens with the leaves on its side of the tree, so an even canopy builds an even nebari (the flare of surface roots). Roots fill the pot over two to three years and then growth slows. Repot between Feb 1 and Apr 10: the tree lifts out so you can cut the taproot or downward roots and swing roots into gaps around the trunk. Cut roots sprout new fine roots that spring. Cutting more than about a third of the root mass at once costs health. The Nebari score rates the spread and thickness of the surface roots.
  • Wood thickens with the leaf mass it feeds, so each part of the trunk thickens according to the branches above it. Let the tree grow wild to fatten the trunk, or leave one low sacrifice branch unpruned to thicken the trunk below it, then cut it off.

Controls: drag to rotate · right-drag or shift-drag to pan · scroll to zoom · Q/E tilt · ←/→ turn 45° · F fit. Keys: Space pause · 1 prune · 2 pinch · 3 wire · Ctrl+Z undo. Progress saves automatically in your browser (time stops while the game is closed); use Export for a backup file.
